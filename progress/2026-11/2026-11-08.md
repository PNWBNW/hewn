- What the bump is for: adapter 1.5.0 lowercases a transaction's status and drops a wallet-internal
  handle that only echoed the request id, which Hewn's `isOnChainId` guard already assumed; and

## 2026-11-08 — A name registration Shield called failed, that the chain had accepted

Joshua's new-user walkthrough in Brave (a worker, on a fresh Shield account funded with 10 ALEO):

fallback to the older program.

Read from the chain: no fallback. `register_worker_free` calls the registry's
`register_worker_name` inside the same transaction, and Shield lists each program's step on its own
line. The account (`worker_primary_name_of`) holds `joshua_day.hewn` (the hash Hewn computes for
`joshua_day`, owned by the account, the registrar's plain text set); `joshuaday` was never
registered. Shield had sent the registration, it landed, Shield sent it again and passed on the
second send's error as the result; every later attempt was rejected by the registry's "a worker can
only have one name" (`at1wkhg5ve8…`, rejected, fee only).

Hewn's side: the name steps (the person's `RegisterName` in `chain/work/Profile.tsx`, the
business's in `chain/RegisterBusiness.tsx`) look for a name the account already holds when they
open, but after a failed send they showed the error and never looked again. Now
`registeredAnyway` (`chain/work/registry.ts`) reads the registry after a failed send, and if the
account owns the name it asked for, the step saves it and moves on as a success. With "already
exists in the ledger" it reads up to four times, five seconds apart, since the registry can trail
the error by a block; a decline or any other failure costs one read; a registry that can't be read
leaves the original error. Joshua's account needs nothing re-sent: reloading the setup page finds
the name on its first check.
