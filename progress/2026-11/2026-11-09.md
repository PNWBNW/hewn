## 2026-11-09 — Twin-curve math is not Dual_EC_DRBG; the technical paper says so

Joshua searched "twin elliptic curve cryptography", got Dual_EC_DRBG (the random-number generator
NIST standardised in 2006 and withdrew in 2014 over its backdoor) and asked whether Hewn's twin-curve
math was the same thing. It isn't. Dual_EC_DRBG is a generator built on two fixed points on one
curve with a hidden relationship between them; twin-curve math is two standard constructions on
two curves, Aleo's record encryption and an ECDH key agreement on P-256 in Web Crypto followed by
HKDF-SHA256 (`chain/coworkers.ts`), with no second point and every key made on the device; Hewn's
randomness is `crypto.getRandomValues` throughout (vault, envelopes, ids). The name invites the
confusion, so `docs/PAPER.md` §4 now says in one paragraph what twin-curve math is not, naming
Dual_EC_DRBG so that a search lands on the right explanation. Joshua chose to keep the name.
X25519 in place of P-256 is open for later; it would touch the public halves `employer_agreement_v5`
carries, so it is a Leo change, flagged first when it comes (INV-22).
