nothing explains: an entry posted to the Shield account by hand, or a movement the feed hasn't found
yet). Movements excluded on purpose and the fraction of a cent the books drop on each accepted
movement and on the opening balance are accounted for, not flagged. The Dashboard's Shield card
(`chain/AccountBalances.tsx`) shows it through `books/ShieldCheck.tsx`: a quiet line when the two

## 2026-10-26 — Keeping the books: the browser is asked to keep them, and backups are asked for

Joshua's two accounts, both made in Shield's in-app browser on his phone, stopped opening ("That
username and passphrase don't match an account on this device"), and setting up again said the
wallet already held a name. Shield had updated around 2026-10-23, and he hadn't logged in since.
The code was ruled out first: a vault made with the code from before the recovery key (2026-10-24)
opens with today's, with and without a recovery key, and nothing in the storage layer has changed
since 2026-10-14. The update emptied Shield's browser storage, and the books with it; nothing on
chain was lost. Threat model T19.

Two changes, so it can't silently happen again:
- **The browser is asked to keep the books.** `@pnw/vault` `requestPersistence()` asks for
  `navigator.storage.persist()` whenever an account is made, opened, recovered or restored on a
  device; Settings shows the answer ("agreed to keep", "may clear", "can't say"). It helps in Chrome
  and Safari; it can't stop an app wiping its own browser, which is why the next part exists.
- **Backups are asked for.** A reminder at the top of the Dashboard and the worker's Home when no
  backup was ever saved, or the last is a week old; a Backups card in Settings with the last
  backup's date. The time is kept in the vault (`Company.backedUpAt`), never in plain browser
  storage. On a phone, "Save a backup" (renamed from "Download a backup") opens the share sheet,
  since a plain download often goes nowhere inside an app's browser; closing the sheet without
  saving doesn't count as a backup.

Checked headless against the website build, on a restored finished business account: the
reminder shows, saving downloads the file on a computer, the reminder then goes, and Settings shows
the date and the browser's answer; no page errors. `pnpm test` 1232/1232 (147 files). Next, if Joshua agrees: a design for an
encrypted backup of the books on chain, restorable on any device with the wallet, username and
passphrase.

## 2026-10-26 — Design: the books backed up on chain (for review)

`docs/CHAIN_BACKUP.md`. Shield's browser lost the books in an update and can't save a file, so on a
phone the only backup that can work leaves through the wallet: letters to the account's own account
on `hewn_letters_v2` (no new Leo), restored on any device with the wallet plus the username and
passphrase or the recovery key. Joshua chose the shape: compressed before it's encrypted, then only
the changes. A backup is a chain of a snapshot and changes, each naming the hash of the state it
builds on, so a missing piece stops a restore cleanly; a new snapshot after half a snapshot of
changes or 12 changes. Each chain has its own content key, wrapped under the passphrase and under
the recovery key; the finder lookups come from the username and the address, so a recovery-key
restore can find them. Measured with the app's own ledger: 100 entries compress to 7.8 KB (one
page), 500 to 34 KB, 2,000 to 133 KB (about 7.5×, half of what's left being entry hashes the
restore can recompute). Receipt files stay in file backups. Limits stated: the wallet is needed,
a passphrase change starts a new chain, ciphertext on a public chain is public for good. Build
order: the format, writing, finding and restoring, then a network test. Joshua's decisions, the same day:
the snapshot rule as proposed; ask weekly, after each pay run and on closing each month; show the
cost before each backup.

## 2026-10-26 — The on-chain backup's format, built (part 1 of 4)

`docs/CHAIN_BACKUP.md`, first of four pull requests: the format, pure, with no wallet and no screen.

- **`@pnw/vault` key slots.** `keySlotsOf(record)` is the data key's two wrapped copies (the
  passphrase's, and the recovery key's once there is one) and nothing of the books.
  `openKeySlots` and `openKeySlotsWithRecovery` open them into a session that writes the books into
  a vault with the very same keys. 3 tests.
- **`books/books_backup.ts`, the pieces.** A snapshot of the company document, then changes (the
  new journal entries as a plain list, other sections whole when they changed), each naming the hash
  of the state it builds on (canonical JSON, keys sorted). Entry hashes are left out and computed
  again on the way back, and the head hash must match. `rebuild` stops at a missing or out-of-place
  piece and keeps the books as of the piece before. The snapshot rule is in `wantsSnapshot`. 7 tests.
- **`chain/books_backup.ts`, the wire.** Each piece is compressed and sealed with the books' data
  key the way a receipt file is (bound to `bb-<chain>-<seq>`). A snapshot carries the key slots, and
  pieces are cut into pages that fit one letter or one page, with lookups from a finder key (username
  and address, never the passphrase). `planBackup` gives the next piece, its pages and their cost;
  `restoreBackup` finds the newest chain whose snapshot is whole and falls back to the one before
  when it isn't. 7 tests: round trips, changes, big books in pages, wrong passphrase refused,
  recovery key, another username or account finds nothing, a missing page stops cleanly, and a new
  chain after 12 changes with fallback.

**A change from the design, recorded in it:** pieces are sealed with the books' own data key, and
the snapshot carries the vault's key slots, rather than a separate content key wrapped twice. The
app never holds the recovery code after showing it once, so it couldn't wrap a new key under it;
the vault's recovery slot already does. Measured with the built code: a new business's books are one
letter (0.0066 ALEO), 100 entries one page, 500 entries two, 2,000 entries seven pages (about 0.53
ALEO). Leaving out the entry hashes took 2,000 entries from 17 pages to 7. `pnpm test` 1249/1249 (150 files).
